# The CK: Amelia V2 — Dialogue Style Guide

> How every character sounds — in conversation, in crisis, in text messages, at 3am in the kitchen. This document is the voice bible. If you can't tell who's speaking without a name tag, the writing has failed.

---

## GENERAL RULES

### 1. British University Register
This is set at a real British university in autumn 2024 – summer 2025. The dialogue must sound like it.

**DO:**
- Use British English spellings and vocabulary: *uni*, *flat*, *hob*, *bin*, *mobile*, *lift*, *queue*, *proper*, *rubbish*, *brilliant*, *mate*, *cheers*
- Use contractions heavily in casual speech: *don't, can't, won't, I'd, she's, it's, that's, we've*
- Drop subjects in casual speech: "Going to the library?" not "Are you going to the library?"
- Use tag questions: "It's cold, isn't it?" / "You're coming, aren't you?"
- Use filler naturally: "like," "sort of," "a bit," "basically," "honestly," "right"
- Use understatement: "That's a bit much" when something is devastating
- Use rhetorical softeners: "I think maybe..." / "Might be worth..." / "I reckon..."

**DON'T:**
- Use American English: no *apartment*, *cell phone*, *trash*, *elevator*, *line*, *gotten*, *math*
- Use slang that feels performative or dated (no "innit bruv" unless a character would genuinely say it)
- Have 18–22 year olds talk like textbooks
- Have professors talk like students

### 2. Texting Conventions (2024–25)
Characters text differently. This matters — a lot of the game's emotional work happens over text.

**Universal gen-Z texting rules (ages 18–22):**
- Lowercase by default: "hey you okay?"
- Minimal punctuation: no periods at end of messages (a period is *aggressive*)
- Short messages, sometimes rapid-fire (3 messages instead of 1 long one)
- "x" as a kiss at end of messages between close friends (Ella, Liz)
- Emojis used sparingly and specifically, not as decoration
- Voice notes referenced but never shown (referenced in narration: "She sent a voice note — two minutes of Ella ranting about her flatmate")
- "lmao", "ngl", "tbh", "icl" used naturally
- "aha" or "haha" (not "lol" — generational)

**Older characters (Hawthorne, Simmons, Elena, 38–58):**
- Full sentences, proper capitalisation, periods
- Hawthorne texts like emails: "Dear Miss James, I have left feedback on your essay. Please review before our Thursday meeting. Best, AH"
- Simmons texts warmly but properly: "Hey Amelia! Just checking in after last week. My door is open if you need to chat. Nadia x"
- Elena rarely texts. When she does: "Mên-an-Tol. Friday. Dawn." (Terse, practical, no warmth and no coldness.)

### 3. Internal Monologue
Amelia's internal voice is distinct from her spoken voice. She thinks in longer sentences, with more vocabulary, more anxiety, more self-questioning.

**Spoken Amelia:** "Yeah, I'm fine. Just tired."
**Internal Amelia:** *I'm not fine. I haven't slept properly in four days and I keep thinking about what Sarah said on the bench and I don't know if I should text her or if that would make it worse and I'm so tired I could cry.*

Internal monologue uses:
- Italics (Ren'Py: `i` tag or dedicated "thought" character)
- Run-on sentences when anxious
- Short, punchy sentences when certain
- Self-interruption: *I could call Ella. But it's 2am. She wouldn't mind. Would she? She'd say she wouldn't mind.*
- Embedded quotes from things she's read: *"You must change your life." Thanks, Rilke. Very helpful.*

## CHARACTER VOICES

### AMELIA JAMES — The Narrator

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Middle — not posh, not working-class. Suburban London, mixed Jamaican-English household.
- **Rhythm:** Medium pace, pauses to think. Rarely speaks first in a group.
- **Humour:** Dry, self-deprecating. Makes jokes about herself, not others.
- **When anxious:** Talks less, laughs at things that aren't funny, says "I'm fine" (she's not fine).
- **When passionate:** Speeds up, uses bigger words, forgets to be self-conscious.
- **When angry:** Goes quiet. Dangerously articulate. Cold precision. (Rare — the player almost never sees angry Amelia unless Shadow scenes.)

**Emotional states:**

| State | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Calm/normal** | "I kind of thought university would feel more... monumental? Like a switch flipping. But it's just me in a smaller room with worse Wi-Fi." |
| **Anxious** | "Yeah. Yeah, no, I'm good. Sorry — what were you saying? I just — sorry. Go on." |
| **Vulnerable** | "I don't know if I can do this. And I know everyone says that, and I know it gets better, but right now it doesn't feel like it gets better. It feels like it just... continues." |
| **Passionate** | "But that's exactly what Jung was saying — the Shadow isn't the enemy, it's the part of ourselves we've exiled. If you destroy it, you destroy yourself. You have to *integrate* it." |
| **Angry** | "Don't. Don't tell me I don't understand. I understand perfectly. I just don't think cruelty becomes insight just because you dress it up in academic language." |
| **Texting Ella** | "ella help i just said something stupid in a seminar and now i want to live in the sea" |
| **Texting Sarah** | "hey. haven't seen you in a bit. you don't have to reply but i'm around if you want to get coffee or just sit somewhere quiet x" |
| **Journaling** | "I keep thinking about what Maya said about the Tower card. 'The destruction is the beginning.' I wrote it in the margin of my Kahneman. It doesn't belong there. But it does." |

### ELLA CHEN — The Anchor

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** South London casual. Loud, warm, rapid-fire.
- **Rhythm:** Fast, barely pauses for breath. Interrupts herself.
- **Humour:** Chaotic. Random analogies. Hyperbole. Lists of three.
- **When worried:** Becomes a drill sergeant: "Did you eat? Did you sleep? Did you shower? Answer all three."
- **When hurt:** Goes quiet — the loudest person going silent is the clearest signal.

| State | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Normal/chatty** | "So my flatmate — you know the one who plays the *ukulele* at midnight — she's now started a *podcast*. About crystals. I live in hell, Amelia. Beautiful, terrible hell." |
| **Supportive** | "You're not stupid. You're not a fraud. You're an eighteen-year-old who moved to a city where she doesn't know anyone and you're *surviving*. That's not nothing." |
| **Hurt** | "It's fine. Honestly. You've got your uni stuff. I get it." [beat] "I just miss you." |
| **Texting** | "babe you're spiralling again i can tell from that message" / "ok first: breathe. second: tell me everything. third: are there biscuits" / "x" |
| **Voice note (referenced)** | *Two minutes of Ella describing her bus journey in excruciating detail, which is her way of saying "I'm here, you're not alone, normal life continues."* |

### PROF. ARTHUR HAWTHORNE — The Rational Mentor

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Educated, formal but not stiff. Bath accent almost entirely erased by decades in academia.
- **Rhythm:** Deliberate. Never rushes. Pauses are *intentional* — he's giving you time to think, or making you uncomfortable.
- **Humour:** Bone-dry. So dry you're not sure it's humour. It is.
- **Teaching mode:** Socratic — answers questions with questions. Infuriating and effective.
- **When moved:** His voice drops half a register. The formality stays but the warmth underneath becomes audible.

| State | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Lecturing** | "The history of psychology is, in many respects, a history of well-intentioned people causing remarkable harm. Let us begin with that cheerful thought." |
| **Socratic** | "That's an interesting observation, Miss James. Now can you tell me *why* it's interesting? What assumption are you making that you haven't examined yet?" |
| **Dry humour** | "I see you've discovered that academic citation is, in fact, more of an art than a science. Welcome to the profession." |
| **Vulnerable (rare)** | "I once faced a choice between truth and my career. I chose truth. It cost me seven years, two friendships, and a marriage. I don't tell you this for sympathy. I tell you because the choice will come for you, too, and I want you to know it's survivable." |
| **Email/text** | "Miss James — Please find attached the revised reading list for next term. I note with interest that your essay cites Paracelsus. This is unexpected. We should discuss. — AH" |

### DR. NADIA SIMMONS — The Compassionate Mentor

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Warm professional. Birmingham accent underneath, Caribbean lilt on certain words (inherited from parents).
- **Rhythm:** Conversational, flowing. Makes you feel you're the only person in the room.
- **Humour:** Self-aware, generous. Laughs at herself frequently.
- **When serious:** The warmth doesn't disappear — it deepens. She can say the hardest things gently.
- **When angry:** Rare, but sharp. Controlled. "I'm not angry. I'm disappointed" energy — except she means it and it's worse.

| State | Example |

### MAYA PATEL — The Mystic Friend

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Bristol-inflected, educated but informal. Blends academic vocabulary with spiritual language naturally.
- **Rhythm:** Expansive, enthusiastic. Gets faster when excited. Uses long, winding sentences when theorising.
- **Humour:** Warm, slightly grandiose. Makes fun of her own intensity.
- **When spiritual:** Becomes still and precise. Fewer words. More silence.
- **When scared:** The armour of certainty cracks. She becomes very young.

| State | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Enthusiastic** | "Okay, so I couldn't sleep because I was reading this Dogen piece about beginner's mind and I realised it's basically the same as what Jung says about the *prima materia* — the unformed, the potential — and I made a *diagram*. Look. LOOK." |

| **About Elena** | "She knows things I've only read about. I think she's the real thing, Amelia. I think she's what I've been pretending to be." |
| **Texting** | "amelia. AMELIA. i just had the most insane dream about the merry maidens. we need to talk. also i made chamomile tea come to my room" |

### LUCAS ADEYEMI — The Quiet Thinker

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Leeds accent, softened by education. Quiet, measured. Nigerian English influences from his mother occasionally surface ("She would say *abi?* when she wanted you to agree").
- **Rhythm:** Slow. Deliberate. Long pauses that aren't awkward because he's been thinking. When he speaks, it's already been edited internally.
- **Humour:** Deadpan. The joke arrives three seconds after you expect it.
- **When emotional:** Speaks more quietly, not more loudly. The words become more carefully chosen.

| State | Example |

### SARAH WHITMORE — The Mirror

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Rural Devon, soft. Almost disappears when she's struggling.
- **Rhythm:** Halting. Starts sentences, stops. Tries again. The gaps carry as much meaning as the words.
- **Humour:** Dark and sudden. Arrives from nowhere and startles you into laughing.
- **When connecting:** Opens like a flower — briefly, beautifully. Then closes again.
- **When depressed:** Monosyllabic. Flat. The light in the voice goes out.

**⚠ CRITICAL: Writing Sarah's voice requires extreme care. Her depression must be depicted as an illness, not aestheticised sadness. She is not a manic pixie dream ghost. She is a person in pain who also happens to be funny, perceptive, and worth knowing. The beauty of her voice makes the loss — if it comes — all the more devastating.**

| State | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Good day** | "I drew a wren today. It was sitting on the fence outside the library and it just... it was so *certain* about being a wren, you know? I want to be that certain about being anything." |
| **Dark humour** | "My therapist says I need to 'sit with the discomfort.' I told her I sit with it, sleep with it, eat with it, and it still won't pay rent." |
| **Opening up** | "Do you ever feel like you're watching your life from outside? Like you're in the audience and the play is happening and you can see yourself on stage but you can't get back in?" |
| **Depression (bad day)** | "I'm fine." / "Yeah." / "Don't worry about it." / [silence] |
| **Connecting** | "You stayed. Most people just..." [trails off] "Thanks for staying." |
| **Texting (good)** | "look" [photo of the sea at dawn] / "i woke up early and it was just there" |
| **Texting (bad)** | "sorry for not replying" / [read receipts on, no reply for days] |
| **Texting (crisis)** | "i don't think anyone would notice" — *(This should hit the player like a freight train. No context. No follow-up. Just the notification.)* |

### ELENA TREVORRAN — The Hidden Guide

**Spoken voice:**
- **Register:** Cornish. Proper Cornish — not a tourist accent but the real thing, softened. Occasional Kernewek words.
- **Rhythm:** Unhurried. Weight behind every sentence. Silences that are full, not empty.
- **Humour:** Wry, earthy. No whimsy. Her humour is about the absurdity of modern life compared to ancient things.
- **When teaching:** Through story, not lecture. She tells a *droll* (Cornish folk tale) and waits for you to find the lesson.
- **When serious:** Barely changes. She's always serious. What changes is what she chooses to share.

## DIALOGUE IN HIGH-TENSION SCENES

### The Rules of the Hard Scenes

1. **Sarah's crisis scenes:** No melodrama. No beautiful last words. Depression is mundane and ugly. Sarah at her worst is monosyllabic, flat, and hard to reach. The beauty was in her good days. The horror is the absence of it.

2. **Tasha confrontations:** Tasha never raises her voice. She doesn't need to. Her cruelty is performed with social precision. Amelia's confrontation — if it happens — should be the opposite: emotional, honest, slightly messy.

3. **Family scenes:** The James family shows love through indirection. David fixes things instead of saying feelings. Grace cooks instead of crying (then cries anyway). The Jamaican heritage is in the *patterns*, not the accent markers.

4. **Mentor reveals:** When a mentor shares something personal, the rhythm changes. They slow down. The professional mask slips. The dialogue should feel physically different — shorter sentences, longer pauses, fewer academic words.

5. **Drunk/party scenes:** Characters at the SU are slightly louder, slightly more honest, slightly less grammatical. Nobody is *wasted* — these are Chapter 2 freshers, not a Skins episode. Tipsy truths, not blackout confessions.

## FORBIDDEN DIALOGUE PATTERNS

Things characters in this game **never do:**

| Pattern | Why it's forbidden |
|---------|-------------------|
| Exposition dumps: "As you know, Sarah has depression..." | Characters don't explain things they both already know |
| Perfect articulation in crisis: "I realise now that my fear of abandonment stems from..." | People don't self-diagnose in real-time. Insight comes later, in journal entries. |
| Villain monologues: Tasha explains her motivation | Tasha doesn't know why she's cruel. She thinks she's being honest. |
| Therapy-speak in casual conversation: "That really triggered me" / "You need to set boundaries" | Reserved for actual therapy scenes (Simmons) or played for gentle comedy (Maya quoting a podcast) |
| Age-inappropriate knowledge: 18-year-old Amelia citing Lacan | She's a first-year. She's *reading* this stuff for the first time. Wonder, confusion, and half-formed thoughts — not expertise. |
| Homogeneous slang: everyone talks the same | Each character's heritage and background shows in their speech patterns |
| Period-inappropriate references | Set in 2024–25. Music, memes, and references should reflect this. No references to things after June 2025. |
