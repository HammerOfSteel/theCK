# The CK: Amelia V2 — World & Locations

> Every location in this game serves three purposes: it is a real place, it is a stage for emotional beats, and it carries a hidden symbolic resonance. The player should feel the rain, smell the coffee, hear the gulls — and *underneath* that, sense something older and stranger.

---

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **Specificity over generality** — not "a café" but "a café on the Barbican with steamed-up windows and a chalkboard menu that always has one item crossed out"
2. **Weather is a character** — Plymouth is one of the rainiest cities in England; Cornwall's mist, storms, and sudden sun are narrative tools
3. **Sound design matters** — each location has a distinct soundscape (seagulls for Plymouth, traffic for London, silence-then-wind for Bodmin Moor)
4. **The mundane and the mystical coexist** — a standing stone is also a place where students drink cider at night; a holy well has a crisp packet floating in it
5. **Locations unlock emotions** — where Amelia goes affects how she feels; the game should direct her to locations that amplify the current narrative beat

---

## LONDON — The Known World

London is HOME: safe, familiar, slightly suffocating. It represents what Amelia must leave in order to grow. When she returns, it feels both comforting and smaller.

### Amelia's Home — Bromley
- **Feel:** Warm, cluttered, lived-in. Smells of cooking and washing powder. Family photos on every surface. Her room still has posters from sixth form.
- **Narrative use:** Opening scenes, phone-call backdrop, return chapters
- **Symbolic:** The *prima materia* — the raw, unworked self; the place you must leave to begin the Work
- **Sensory:** Sound of the kettle, Radio 4 in the kitchen, her dad humming while he fixes things, foxes screaming at night

### Ella's Flat — Bermondsey
- **Feel:** Tiny, messy, full of character. Fairy lights, books stacked on the floor, a cat called Plath.
- **Narrative use:** Phone calls, visit during Chapter 10 (Road Back)
- **Symbolic:** The mirror — Ella's life is the road not taken; what would Amelia be if she'd stayed?

### Thames Riverside — Southbank
- **Feel:** The vast river, grey and brown, tourists and joggers, the Tate Modern looming. Pigeons. The sound of buskers.
- **Narrative use:** Contemplation scenes, chapter openings/closings
- **Symbolic:** The waters of the unconscious — always flowing, carrying things away

### Bookshop — Bromley
- **Feel:** Narrow, overstuffed, carpet worn thin. The owner knows Amelia by name. A shelf in the back labelled "Esoterica" that nobody ever seems to buy from.
- **Narrative use:** Chapter 1 — Amelia finds a book that plants the seed for the occult path. A copy of Paracelsus or the *Corpus Hermeticum* with a note tucked inside.
- **Symbolic:** The threshold — knowledge waits in plain sight for those who look

## PLYMOUTH — The Arena of Transformation

Plymouth is where the Work happens. It is wet, grey, beautiful in unexpected ways, and full of students trying to figure out who they are. The city has a naval history — Drake, the Armada, the Mayflower — and an underbelly of poverty and resilience that contrasts with the university bubble.

### University of Plymouth — Campus
- **Feel:** Brutalist architecture softened by green spaces. The Roland Levinsky building is striking; the older blocks are not. Students everywhere — on benches, in corridors, sprawled on grass when the sun appears. The wind off the Sound hits you crossing campus.
- **Key sub-locations:**
  - **The Psych Building** — where Amelia spends most of her time. Lecture theatres, seminar rooms, the departmental coffee machine that charges 40p and delivers despair.
  - **The Library** — five floors. Amelia's favourite spot is the fourth floor by the window overlooking the city. At night the lights of the Hoe reflect on the water.
  - **The SU (Student Union)** — loud, sticky floors, cheap pints. Karaoke on Thursdays. Where friendships are forged and tested.
  - **Halls of Residence** — standard en-suite rooms, communal kitchen. The kitchen is the social hub — tea, arguments, 2am toast. This is where Amelia meets Liz, encounters Lucas at 3am eating cereal, first sees Sarah sitting alone.

### Plymouth Hoe
- **Feel:** The wide green promenade above the sea. Smeaton's Tower lighthouse, red and white. Drake's statue, pigeons on his head. The Sound stretching out, cold and grey-blue. On clear days you can see the breakwater and the edge of Cornwall.
- **Narrative use:** Key emotional scenes — arguments, revelations, quiet moments. This is where characters go to think.
- **Symbolic:** The **Watchtower** — the place between land and sea, between the known and the unknown. Standing here, you can see where you came from and where you're going.
- **Sensory:** Wind always, seagulls screaming, the ice cream van that plays its song even in November, distant foghorns

### The Barbican
- **Feel:** Cobblestoned narrow streets, pubs dating back centuries, the fishing quay with its lobster pots and diesel smell. The Mayflower Steps — a plaque marking where the Pilgrims left for America. Tourist tat shops next to genuine maritime heritage.
- **Key sub-locations:**
  - **The Anchor pub** — group hangout; cheap food, sticky tables, live music on Fridays
  - **Second-hand bookshop** — where Amelia finds texts that deepen the occult path
  - **The fish market at dawn** — a scene of raw, unglamorous beauty; Maya drags Amelia here to "see reality before philosophy"
- **Symbolic:** The **departure point** — historically, this is where people left the known world; thematically, each Barbican visit takes Amelia further from who she was

### Royal William Yard
- **Feel:** Converted naval buildings, now galleries and restaurants. Dramatic stone architecture against water. Quieter than the Barbican — more contemplative.
- **Narrative use:** Mentor conversations (especially Hawthorne, who likes to walk here). Art exhibitions that mirror Amelia's internal state.
- **Symbolic:** **Transformation of purpose** — buildings designed for war now house art and food; the past repurposed, not erased

### Prof. Hawthorne's Office
- **Feel:** The door is always slightly ajar. Floor-to-ceiling books, categorised by era. A green banker's lamp. Earl Grey in a bone china cup — always. A print of Caravaggio's *The Incredulity of Saint Thomas* on the wall. One small window overlooking the car park (he doesn't care about the view; the view is in the books).
- **Narrative use:** Mentor scenes, academic crises, the moment Hawthorne reveals his own past
- **Symbolic:** The **laboratory** — the place of careful, methodical work; the opposite of intuition, and therefore its necessary complement

### Dr. Simmons' Office
- **Feel:** Plants. So many plants. A small fountain on her desk that makes a trickling sound. Motivational posters that are *almost* corny but somehow work because she means them. Comfortable chairs — she chose them herself because "people can't heal in uncomfortable chairs."
- **Narrative use:** Mental health check-ins, the Sarah crisis, Simmons' own vulnerability reveal
- **Symbolic:** The **garden** — a cultivated space where things are encouraged to grow

## CORNWALL — The Otherworld

Cornwall is where the veil thins. It is not a separate world; it is *this* world seen differently. The landscape itself is the teacher — ancient, indifferent, beautiful, dangerous. Cornwall trips happen at key narrative moments and escalate in intensity and weirdness depending on the player's path.

### General Feel
- **Wild** — even the "pretty" parts have an edge; the cliffs are real cliffs, the sea is really cold, the mist really does descend in minutes
- **Ancient** — the granite is 300 million years old; the standing stones predate writing; the tin mines go back to the Bronze Age
- **Liminal** — Cornwall is the end of the land; everything here is at a boundary — land/sea, earth/sky, past/present, real/imagined
- **Cornish identity** — this is not England in the way Surrey is England; the Cornish language exists, the flag flies, the people have a distinct sense of place

### St. Ives
- **Feel:** Impossible light — painters have been coming here for a century because the light is like nowhere else in Britain. Narrow streets of whitewashed cottages. Galleries. Pasty shops. Turquoise sea that looks Mediterranean until you touch it.
- **Narrative use:** Early Cornwall trip (any mentor path). Art and beauty as healing.
- **OK path extra:** The Tate St Ives has an exhibition on alchemy in art — copper, tin, transformation of base materials. Elena points at a painting and says "That's not a metaphor."

### Tintagel
- **Feel:** Dramatic cliff-top ruins. The castle is mostly gone, but the setting is extraordinary — spray, wind, nesting seabirds, the cave below (Merlin's Cave, supposedly). New footbridge spanning the gap over the sea. Arthurian legend soaks everything.
- **Narrative use:** Maya's path — she brings Amelia here to talk about myth as psychology. "Arthur isn't a person. Arthur is a *pattern*."
- **OK path extra:** Elena reveals that the Arthurian legends encode alchemical processes — the Grail is the *lapis philosophorum*, the Round Table is the *opus circulatorium*
- **Symbolic:** The **threshold between worlds** — you cross the bridge to reach the castle; you leave the mainland behind

### Bodmin Moor
- **Feel:** Vast, open, treeless moorland. Granite tors rising like bones from the earth. Wild ponies. The wind is constant and ancient. At night, total darkness — no light pollution — and the stars are overwhelming. The Cheesewring (a natural rock formation that looks impossible), Dozmary Pool (where Excalibur was supposedly returned), the Beast of Bodmin legends.
- **Narrative use:** Deep emotional scenes — characters are stripped bare by the landscape. No hiding places on a moor.
- **OK path extra:** Elena takes Amelia to The Hurlers — a stone circle where legend says men were turned to stone for playing games on the Sabbath. "Or," Elena says, "they chose to become stone. To hold a pattern in place."
- **Symbolic:** The **Nigredo landscape** — the dark, stripped-down place where the old self dies

### Mên-an-Tol
- **Feel:** Three stones on open moorland near Madron — two uprights and a round stone with a hole you can crawl through. Small, unassuming, impossibly ancient. No fences, no gift shop, just a path through the heather.
- **Narrative use:** OK path — Elena's key location. The tradition: crawl through the holed stone for healing, for fertility, for transformation. "You go through as one thing. You come out as another."
- **Symbolic:** The **Athanor opening** — the portal through which the alchemical transformation is entered; death and rebirth in miniature

### Madron Holy Well & Baptistry
- **Feel:** Hidden in the woods near Madron village. A ruined stone baptistry, roofless, with a crude stone altar. Water seeps from the ground. Clooties (rags) tied to the trees by visitors seeking healing — hundreds of them, faded by sun and rain. Quiet. Very quiet.
- **Narrative use:** OK path — Amelia visits with Elena or alone. A place of genuine, uncanny power.
- **Symbolic:** The **albedo pool** — purification, baptism, the washing away of what is no longer needed

### St. Michael's Mount
- **Feel:** The tidal island rising from Mount's Bay — castle on top, gardens cascading down. At low tide you walk the cobbled causeway; at high tide, you're cut off. The symmetry with Mont-Saint-Michel is not a coincidence — the St. Michael line runs through both.
- **Narrative use:** Simmons' path — she takes Amelia here to talk about perspective ("You can see everything from up there, but you can also get trapped when the tide comes in"). Also used in the OK path — the ley line / St. Michael line significance.
- **Symbolic:** The **island of the self** — separate from the world, elevated, but also isolated; accessible only at certain times

### Lost Gardens of Heligan
- **Feel:** A vast garden left to ruin for decades, then lovingly restored. The Jungle section — giant rhubarb, tree ferns, a sense of being in another world entirely. The Mud Maid sculpture sleeping in the earth, covered in living moss and plants.
- **Narrative use:** Simmons' path — "This is what restoration looks like. Not going back to what was, but growing something new from what survived."
- **Symbolic:** The **albedo garden** — the return of green life after the nigredo devastation

### Eden Project
- **Feel:** Two enormous biome domes in a former clay pit — tropical rainforest and Mediterranean. The contrast between the grey Cornish exterior and the lush interior is startling. Educational, a bit corporate, but genuinely impressive.
- **Narrative use:** Group trip early in the year — lighthearted, bonding, comedic moments (Raj in the tropical dome: "This is what I was promised when I came to uni. Where has this weather been hiding?")
- **Symbolic:** **Created paradise** — humanity's attempt to contain and display nature; beautiful but artificial; a question about what is real

### Polperro & Fowey
- **Feel:** Tiny fishing villages, impossibly narrow lanes, cottages pressed together, harbour water slapping against stone. Polperro is smaller and sleepier; Fowey has Daphne du Maurier connections and a slightly literary air.
- **Narrative use:** Quiet character moments — Amelia and Lucas walk through Polperro and talk about their fathers; Amelia and Ella meet in Fowey during a visit and sit on the harbour wall eating chips.
- **Symbolic:** **The village** — community at its smallest scale; the opposite of the university's anonymity

### Penzance
- **Feel:** The last major town before Land's End. Victorian promenade, slightly faded grandeur, independent shops and galleries. The Jubilee Pool — an Art Deco lido right on the sea. The Egyptian House on Chapel Street (genuinely bizarre architecture).
- **Narrative use:** Elena's home territory — her flat above a shop on Chapel Street, full of dried herbs and books in Cornish. The scene where Elena reveals her true nature.
- **Symbolic:** **The end of the road** — you can't go much further west in England; this is the edge before the edge

### Fogou (underground chambers)
- **Feel:** Ancient stone-lined underground passages, purpose unknown. Dark, cool, earthy. Possibly ritual spaces, possibly storage. The most accessible is at Carn Euny near Sancreed. You have to crouch to enter. The silence inside is total.
- **Narrative use:** OK path climax — Elena's final test. Amelia enters alone, with a candle. What she experiences inside depends on her accumulated stats and choices.
- **Symbolic:** The **crucible** — the sealed vessel in which the final transformation occurs; the belly of the whale; the descent to the underworld

## CORNISH LANGUAGE & FOLKLORE INTEGRATION

The game subtly weaves Cornish language and folklore into the world:

- **Kernewek phrases** scattered in Elena's dialogue and on signs in Cornwall: *Dydh da* (good day), *Meur ras* (thank you), *An Gof* (the blacksmith — historical rebel), *Kernow bys vyken* (Cornwall forever)
- **Piskies** — Cornish faeries, mischievous but not malicious; Elena references them matter-of-factly ("Mind the piskies on the moor — they'll turn you around if you don't pay attention")
- **Drolls** — Cornish folk tales, told by Elena, each containing an encoded alchemical lesson
- **The Cornish Chough** — the red-billed crow, symbol of Cornwall; appears at significant moments like a living omen
- **Tin mining heritage** — the engine houses dotting the cliffs are monuments to transformation: ore into metal, earth into value, pain into purpose
- **The Merry Maidens** — stone circle near Penzance; nineteen stones, supposedly girls turned to stone for dancing on a Sunday. Elena: "Nineteen women chose to stop. To hold still. There's power in that."
