# The CK: Amelia V2 — Sarah's Arc (Detailed Outline)

> The most sensitive thread in the game, given its own document. Sarah Whitmore's storyline deals with depression, self-harm, and suicidal ideation. Every scene, outcome, and line of dialogue in this arc must be handled with care, accuracy, and compassion.

---

## CONTENT WARNING APPROACH

### In-Game Implementation
1. **Game start:** A content warning screen before the first scene, listing themes including depression and suicide. Player can toggle "sensitive content mode" which adds per-scene warnings.
2. **Per-scene warnings (opt-in):** Before Sarah's most intense scenes (Ch3 bench, Ch6 room visit, Ch7 darkness visible, Ch8 crisis), a gentle interstitial: "This scene contains depictions of depression [and suicidal ideation]. Helpline numbers are available in the settings menu."
3. **Settings menu:** Real helpline numbers always accessible:
   - **Samaritans (UK):** 116 123 (24hr, free)
   - **Shout Crisis Text Line:** Text SHOUT to 85258
   - **CALM (Campaign Against Living Miserably):** 0800 58 58 58
   - **Papyrus (under-35 suicide prevention):** 0800 068 4141
4. **Post-crisis scene (all outcomes):** An in-world text-graphic: "If anything in this chapter affected you, please reach out to [helpline]." This is not breaking the fourth wall — it's responsible design.

### Writing Principles
- **Depression is an illness, not a character trait.** Sarah is funny, perceptive, artistic, and kind. She also has depression. These are not contradictions.
- **No romanticisation.** Sarah's suffering is ugly, mundane, and exhausting — not beautiful or poetic. Her good moments are the beautiful ones.
- **No "right answer" framing.** The game does not suggest that the player's choices could have "fixed" Sarah. If Sarah dies, it is not the player's fault. The game explicitly addresses this.
- **Informed by reality.** Sarah's symptoms, behaviours, and trajectory follow clinical patterns for severe depression in young adults, reviewed against NHS and Mind guidance.
- **The spectrum, not the binary.** Sarah's fate exists on a 4-tier continuum, not alive/dead. Each tier is its own story with its own emotional truth.

## SARAH'S BACKGROUND (Not All Revealed to Player)

### What Amelia Learns (Gradually)
- From rural Devon, small village outside Exeter
- Only child of older parents (father a farmer, mother a librarian)
- Strong academically — got into Plymouth on merit
- Quiet, artistic, writes poetry she doesn't share
- Has a wren tattoo on her inner wrist (she drew it herself)
- Mentions a previous counsellor "back home" (brief, easy to miss)
- Sometimes says things that suggest she's been depressed before

### What the Player Never Sees (Authorial Knowledge)
- Sarah's depression began at 14 after a period of intense bullying at school
- Her parents are loving but emotionally unequipped — her father doesn't believe in mental illness ("just needs fresh air"), her mother is anxious and overprotective
- She was on SSRIs from 16 to 18, then stopped before university (common and dangerous)
- She applied to Plymouth specifically to get away from home
- She has self-harmed in the past (cutting), though this is in remission when the game begins
- She genuinely wants to get better. Depression tells her she doesn't deserve to.

## SARAH SCENE-BY-SCENE

### Chapter 1: Absent
Sarah does not appear. No mention. This is Devon, not London.

---

### Chapter 2: First Meeting
**Scene:** The kitchen, move-in day. Sarah is already there when everyone arrives. She's sitting at the table with a cup of tea, drawing in the margin of a notebook.

**What the player sees:**
- She's quiet. Not unfriendly — just still. In a room where Raj is cooking and Liz is talking about dolphins, Sarah is the pause.
- If Amelia approaches her (Choice 2.4C): Sarah looks up, surprised. "Oh — hi. Sorry, I was just... it doesn't matter. I'm Sarah." A half-smile. Warm but fragile.
- If Amelia doesn't approach her: Sarah is in the background. You might not notice her at all.

**What's happening underneath:**
- Sarah is in the early weeks of a university-onset depressive episode, exacerbated by stopping medication and leaving home
- She is functioning — attending, eating, polite — but only just
- The flat's warmth (Raj's cooking, Liz's energy) is keeping her tethered

**Tone:** Light. Natural. No ominous music. No foreshadowing shots. Sarah is a normal girl at university who happens to be fighting something invisible.

---

### Chapter 3: The Bench — "The Crack Opens"
**Scene:** Plymouth Hoe, late October. Amelia and Sarah sitting on a bench looking at the sea. Mid-afternoon, grey sky, cold.

**How they got here:** They bumped into each other after a lecture. Sarah was going to walk to the Hoe. Amelia came along.

**The conversation:**
The talk is normal at first — lectures, weather, whether the library café is overpriced. Then Sarah goes quiet. Amelia notices.

> SARAH: "Do you ever feel like you're watching your life from outside? Like you're in the audience and the play is happening and you can see yourself on stage but you can't get back in?"

This is the defining moment. Three response options:

**A) Honest vulnerability:** "Yeah... I think I do sometimes."
- Sarah's face changes. Relief. Someone *understands*.
- They sit in silence for a while. It's not uncomfortable.
- Sarah: "Thanks for not telling me it's just stress."
- `{Sarah +2}` — this is the biggest single relationship gain in the game

**B) Clinical care:** "That sounds really hard. Do you want to talk about it?"
- Sarah appreciates the concern but closes slightly. "It's fine. I'm fine. Just thinking out loud."
- The crack opened. But Amelia approached it as a problem to solve, not a feeling to share.
- `{Sarah +1}`

**C) Dismissal:** "I'm sure it'll pass. University is stressful for everyone."
- Sarah nods. "Yeah. You're right." The light goes out of her eyes.
- She changes the subject. She doesn't raise it again.
- `{Sarah -1}`

**Tone:** Quiet. Natural. Two people on a bench. The sea doing most of the talking. No dramatic music. Seagulls and wind.

**What must NOT happen:** No voiceover saying "This seems important." No stat notification. The player must feel this moment, not be told to feel it.

---

### Chapter 4: Peripheral
**Scene:** Sarah is not central to this chapter (it's the mentor chapter). But she's there if you look.

**One choice available (4.4A):** Amelia can text Sarah: "Hey, haven't seen you in lectures. You okay?"
- Sarah replies after 6 hours: "yeah sorry just been a bit off. thanks for checking in x"
- `{Sarah +1}`

**What the player might miss:** Sarah's absence from lectures is the first behavioural sign. She was in every lecture in Ch2. She's missed three now.

---

### Chapter 5: Background
Sarah is present in group scenes but quieter. If the player has invested, they might notice:
- She's thinner
- She laughs less
- She leaves group events early: "I'm just tired. I'll see you tomorrow."

No direct choice point in this chapter. Sarah is receding. The question is whether the player notices.

---

### Chapter 6: "Go to Her Room" — The Second Test
**Scene:** Week 9. Sarah has missed the last two weeks of lectures. Her texts are short and days apart. Raj mentions he hasn't seen her.

**Choice 6.2 — The Four Responses:**

**A) Go to her room, knock, don't leave.** `[MH +1] [SI +1] {Sarah +2}`
- Amelia walks down the corridor. Knocks. "Sarah? It's me. I'm not going away."
- Silence. Then the door unlocks.
- Sarah's room: curtains closed, unwashed dishes, bed unmade. Sarah is sitting on the bed in yesterday's clothes.
- Amelia doesn't react to the room. She says: "Can I sit down?"
- They don't talk about anything important. Amelia just... stays.
- After an hour, Sarah says: "I think I need help."
- Amelia: "Okay. Let's figure that out."

**B) Text: "I'm here if you need me."** `[SI +1] {Sarah +1}`
- Sarah reads it. Doesn't reply for two days. Then: "thanks x"
- Adequate. But a text is easy to send and easy to ignore. Sarah needed a doorknock.

**C) Mention to counselling services.** `[MC +1] {Sarah +1}`
- Professional, responsible. Counselling reaches out to Sarah. She attends one session and doesn't return.
- The institutional route works for some people. Sarah needed personal connection.

**D) Assume she's busy.** `{Sarah -1}`
- Amelia doesn't follow up. Sarah's absence normalises. The corridor stays empty.
- This is the most common response in real life. It's also the most damaging.

**Tone:** The room visit should feel uncomfortable. Amelia is intruding. Sarah hasn't invited her. But sometimes love is showing up when you haven't been invited.

---

### Chapter 7: "Darkness Visible" — The Alarm
**Scene:** February. A specific, alarming moment that signals immediate danger.

**What Amelia notices (one of these, depending on player's relationship level):**
- `(IF {Sarah} ≥ 5)` Sarah says something in conversation that stops Amelia cold: "I've been thinking about what I'd want people to know. If I wasn't around." Amelia recognises this.
- `(IF {Sarah} 3-4)` A mutual friend (Liz or Raj) says, "Is Sarah okay? She gave me her favourite book yesterday and said 'I want you to have it.'" Red flag: giving away possessions.
- `(IF {Sarah} < 3)` Amelia hears secondhand from a lecturer that Sarah hasn't submitted any work in three weeks.

**Choice 7.2 — The Critical Response:**

**A) Act immediately.** `[MC +1] [MH +1] {Sarah +2}`
- Amelia goes to Sarah. Tells her what she's noticed. Says: "I'm scared for you. I'm going to help you talk to someone."
- Sarah doesn't deny it. She's too tired to deny it. "Okay."
- This is the single highest-impact choice in the entire game for the Sarah Score.

**B) Talk to Sarah privately.** `[SI +1] {Sarah +1}`
- Amelia finds Sarah and tries to have the conversation. Sarah says she's fine. Amelia pushes gently. Sarah half-opens up.
- It helps. But Amelia is not a professional. She's an 18-year-old trying to be a therapist. It's not enough on its own.

**C) Tell a friend.** `[SI +1] {Sarah +1}`
- Amelia shares her concern with Lucas or Zara. They take it seriously. Together they check on Sarah.
- The communal approach. It works — slightly. But it diffuses the urgency.

**D) Convince herself she misread the signs.** `{Sarah -2}`
- Amelia does nothing. "She's probably fine. I'm probably overthinking it."
- **⚠ This is the most catastrophic choice in the game outside of Ch8 itself.** It costs 2 relationship points AND the denial feeds directly into a lower Sarah Score.

### Chapter 8: THE CRISIS

#### Pre-Crisis State
By this point, the Sarah Score has been silently accumulating:

```
Sarah Score = ({Sarah} × 3) + MH + SI + MC + ({Ella} × 0.5)
```

Everything Amelia has done — every small text, every doorknock, every confrontation she avoided, every time she built her own mental health and social capacity — is now a number. And that number determines what happens next.

#### The Event
**What happens is NOT a player choice.** It's a consequence.

Sarah attempts self-harm with suicidal intent. The specifics are not described graphically — the game shows the *discovery*, not the act:
- Amelia comes to check on Sarah (because of Ch7 concerns) or is alerted by someone
- She finds Sarah's door unlocked
- She enters
- What she finds depends on the Sarah Score and the Karma Dice

#### The Four Tiers

---

**TIER 1: FULL SAVE (Sarah Score ≥ 45)**

Amelia finds Sarah *before* the attempt, or at its very beginning.

**What happens:**
- Amelia arrives and immediately recognises the danger
- She calls 999 and stays with Sarah
- An ambulance comes. Sarah is hospitalised for assessment.
- Sarah is angry, relieved, and exhausted. She says: "How did you know?"
- Amelia: "Because I was paying attention."

**Aftermath (Ch8.5):**
- Sarah is in hospital. Amelia visits.
- Their friendship is changed — more serious, more careful, more real.
- Sarah begins proper treatment: medication, therapy, support plan.
- She will not be "fixed" by the end of the game. She will be *in recovery*. That's different.
- She may return to Plymouth next year, or she may defer. The game doesn't resolve this — because real recovery doesn't resolve neatly.

**Emotional tone:** Relief that hurts. The crisis averted is still a crisis. Amelia cries in the hospital car park afterwards.

---

**TIER 2: LATE SAVE (Sarah Score 30–44)**

The attempt has happened. Sarah is found in time. She has harmed herself but will survive.

**What happens:**
- Amelia arrives late — Sarah has already acted
- She calls 999. The ambulance comes. Touch and go.
- Hospital. Sarah survives. She's angry — at herself, at Amelia, at everything.
- "Why did you come? Why couldn't you just leave me alone?"
- Amelia: "Because I'm your friend. And friends don't leave."

**Aftermath:**
- Sarah is in hospital, longer stay than Tier 1
- The friendship is strained. Sarah feels exposed. Amelia feels guilty (she could have come earlier)
- Over weeks, they tentatively rebuild. It's harder. It's slower. It's still worth doing.
- Sarah will likely take time away from university.

**Emotional tone:** Guilt, fear, relief, anger — all at once. Nothing is clean.

---

**TIER 3: PARTIAL SAVE (Sarah Score 15–29)**

Sarah survives but the damage is significant. The friendship may not.

**What happens:**
- Sarah is found by someone else (Raj, Liz, or a cleaner — not Amelia)
- Amelia learns afterwards — a phone call from Liz, or a message in the flat group chat
- Sarah is hospitalised. She survives but she is changed.
- When Amelia visits, Sarah barely looks at her. "Where were you?"

**Aftermath:**
- Sarah leaves Plymouth. She goes home to Devon.
- The friendship is damaged — possibly repairable, but not during the game's timeframe
- Amelia carries this. The game doesn't let her off the hook. But it also doesn't punish her — it shows the weight of what happened and lets the player sit with it.
- In the epilogue, a text from Sarah months later: "I'm okay. Sort of. I hope you are too." It's enough. Barely.

**Emotional tone:** Failure that isn't total failure. The worst kind of "she survived" — the kind where you can't celebrate because too much was lost.

---

**TIER 4: TRAGIC (Sarah Score < 15)**

Sarah dies.

**What happens:**
- Amelia is told. By a counsellor. In a room. The words are said and the world changes.
- The game DOES NOT show Sarah's death. It shows Amelia receiving the news.
- Everything after this scene is different. The music changes. The colour palette shifts. Other characters are affected.
- The group grieves together, or separately, or badly.

**Aftermath (Ch8.4):**
- Amelia's response is a choice — but all options are forms of surviving, not "winning":
  - A) Grieve openly, lean on friends `[MH +1] [SI +1]`
  - B) Channel grief into purpose `[MC +1] [SD +1]`
  - C) Shut down, withdraw `[MH -2] [SI -1]` **⚠ Dangerous — leads toward Grief ending**
- Sarah's absence is felt in every subsequent chapter. Her seat in lectures is empty. Her room is cleared by her parents (Amelia helps, if she can handle it). Raj cooks her favourite meal one night and nobody can eat it.

**Emotional tone:** Devastation. Not dramatic devastation — the quiet, permanent kind. The game continues because life continues. That's the cruelest part.

**The game MUST then include:**
- An explicit acknowledgement that Sarah's death is not the player's fault: via mentor dialogue or journal entry. "You couldn't have saved her. You could have been there. But saving was never in your hands."
- Helpline numbers displayed
- A tone shift that persists through the rest of the game — not as "punishment" but as realism

### Chapters 9–11: Living With It

#### If Sarah Lives (Tiers 1–3)

**Chapter 9:** Amelia processes the crisis. Sarah is either recovering in hospital (T1-2) or gone home (T3).
- T1: A phone call where Sarah sounds tired but present. "I'm going to be okay. I think."
- T2: A text. "i don't hate you. i just need space."
- T3: Silence. Then, weeks later, a short email forwarded by a mutual contact.

**Chapter 10 (London):** Amelia tells her parents. David goes very quiet. Grace holds Amelia's hands. Ella says: "Oh my god. Are *you* okay?"

**Chapter 11:** Sarah is not at the end-of-year celebration. Her absence is noted. Raj raises a glass.

#### If Sarah Died (Tier 4)

**Chapter 9:** The group is fractured. Some people are angry (at the university, at themselves, at each other). Some are numb. The academic recovery scenes are coloured by grief — Amelia writes a better essay because she writes about what she knows now.

**Chapter 10:** London is refuge and ambush. Everything reminds her. Ella's alive and warm and it makes Amelia grateful and furious simultaneously.

**Chapter 11:**
- A memorial moment — the group plants something, or visits a place, or just sits together
- Amelia's resurrection test (11.6) is specifically about facing Sarah's death: can she carry this and still live? Can she be changed by it without being destroyed?
- The Fogou scene (Elena path, if applicable) may include a sense of Sarah's presence — not supernatural, just the kind of thing grief does

## SARAH'S VOICE ACROSS THE ARC

### The Gradient of Depression

| Phase | How Sarah Sounds | Example |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| **Functional (Ch2)** | Quiet but present. Warm edges. Observant. | "The sea looks different here than in Devon. Less... polite." |
| **Opening up (Ch3)** | Tentative honesty. Testing whether it's safe. | "Do you ever feel like you're watching your life from outside?" |
| **Withdrawing (Ch5-6)** | Shorter sentences. Less eye contact. Deflection. | "I'm fine." / "Just tired." / "Yeah." |
| **Crisis approach (Ch7)** | Flatness. The humour is gone. Occasional flashes of something terrifying in its clarity. | "I've been thinking about what I'd want people to know." |
| **Crisis (Ch8)** | Beyond words, mostly. If she speaks, it's either rage or whisper. | "Why couldn't you just leave me alone?" OR "...stay?" |
| **Recovery T1 (Ch9+)** | Slow return. Not the same. More careful. | "I'm going to be okay. I think. Some days I even believe it." |
| **Recovery T2 (Ch9+)** | Fragile. Guarded. Working on it. | "I don't hate you. I just need to figure this out on my own for a bit." |

### What Sarah NEVER says:
- "You saved me" — she is not Amelia's accomplishment
- Anything that frames her depression as a gift or a superpower
- A neat explanation for why she's depressed
- "I'm all better now" — even in the best outcome, she's in recovery, not cured

### What Sarah DOES say (in better moments):
- Things that are funny and dark and surprising
- Observations about beauty — the light, the sea, a bird, a colour
- Questions that nobody else asks: "Why do we pretend everything's fine all the time?"
- Thank yous that come out sideways: *[sends a photo of the wren drawing]* "i made this for you"

## AVOIDING HARMFUL TROPES

| Trope | Why it's harmful | What we do instead |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **"Love cures depression"** | Implies mental illness is a relationship problem | Amelia's care matters, but it doesn't cure Sarah. Professional help does. Amelia is a *factor*, not the factor. |
| **Suicide as drama** | Reduces a person to a plot point | Sarah's crisis exists on a spectrum. The player experiences it through relationship, not spectacle. |
| **Beautiful corpse** | Aestheticises death | If Sarah dies, it is ugly and permanent and the game goes on without her. No beautiful last words. No peaceful expression. |
| **The saviour narrative** | Suggests one person can "save" another | This is explicitly addressed: "You couldn't have saved her. You could have been there." |
| **Depression = depth** | Implies suffering makes you interesting | Sarah is interesting *despite* her depression. Her art, humour, and perception exist alongside her illness, not because of it. |
| **The "wake-up call" death** | Uses a character's death to motivate the protagonist | Sarah's death changes Amelia, but it is NOT framed as "good for her growth." It is a loss. Period. |
| **Recovery as linear** | The "getting better" montage | If Sarah survives, her recovery is non-linear. Good days and bad days. Medication adjustments. Setbacks. The game ends with her *in process*, not finished. |

## THE SARAH SCORE — DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The Sarah Score exists so that the player experiences *consequences without blame*.

### What the score measures:
**Not:** "Did the player make the right choices?"
**Rather:** "What kind of person has Amelia become over these eight chapters? And what kind of environment has she created around Sarah?"

The formula:
```
Sarah Score = ({Sarah} × 3) + MH + SI + MC + ({Ella} × 0.5)
```

- **{Sarah} × 3:** Direct investment in the relationship (weighted most heavily — personal connection matters most)
- **MH:** Amelia's mental health capacity (you can't help others if you're drowning)
- **SI:** Amelia's social skills and network (isolation kills; community saves)
- **MC:** Amelia's moral courage (the willingness to act, not just feel)
- **{Ella} × 0.5:** The anchor. Maintaining her oldest friendship demonstrates a capacity for sustained care. Small weight but real.

### Why it works:
A player who neglects Sarah specifically but has high MH, SI, and MC will still achieve a mid-tier outcome. Because in real life, *the environment matters*. A campus where people care about each other, where mental health is taken seriously, where courage is normalised — that campus saves lives even indirectly.

Conversely, a player with high {Sarah} but low MH and SI might still lose her — because Amelia was so focused on Sarah she neglected herself, and when the crisis came, she didn't have the capacity to act.

The Sarah Score is not a "did you pick the right dialogue options" check. It's a "who have you become?" check. That's the whole point.
